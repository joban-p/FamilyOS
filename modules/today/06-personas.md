# 06 Personas

## Purpose

This document defines the primary household personas that Today must support.

FamilyOS should not assume one traditional family model. Today must work for different household structures, responsibilities, routines and levels of technical confidence.

## Persona 1: The Overloaded Parent

### Profile

A working parent managing school, work, bills, meals, activities, appointments and household admin.

### Needs

- Know what matters today.
- Avoid forgotten school or family responsibilities.
- Coordinate with a partner or co-parent.
- Reduce morning stress.
- Capture tasks quickly.

### Pain points

- Information is spread across school apps, WhatsApp, email, calendar and memory.
- They are often the person who remembers everything.
- Small forgotten tasks create disproportionate stress.

### Today must help by

- Surfacing school, care, money and home responsibilities together.
- Showing one clear priority.
- Making preparation tasks visible before they become urgent.

## Persona 2: The Shared Household Coordinator

### Profile

A person living with a partner, flatmates or extended family where bills, tasks and responsibilities are shared.

### Needs

- See shared responsibilities clearly.
- Know who owns what.
- Avoid awkward chasing.
- Track household money and commitments.

### Pain points

- Shared tasks are unclear.
- Bills and expenses are discussed in messages and then forgotten.
- No one has one trusted view of the household.

### Today must help by

- Showing shared commitments.
- Making responsibility visible.
- Highlighting money and task items that need attention.

## Persona 3: The Co-parent

### Profile

A parent sharing care responsibilities across households.

### Needs

- Know care schedules.
- Avoid confusion about handovers.
- Track child-related events, documents and tasks.
- Keep coordination calm and factual.

### Pain points

- Communication can be sensitive.
- Schedules change.
- Important child-related information is easy to miss.

### Today must help by

- Presenting care commitments clearly.
- Showing child-specific preparation items.
- Reducing the need for repeated messages.

## Persona 4: The Carer

### Profile

Someone helping care for an elderly parent, relative or dependant while managing their own life.

### Needs

- See appointments, medication, visits and care tasks.
- Know what needs attention today.
- Coordinate with siblings, carers or professionals.
- Store important documents safely.

### Pain points

- Care responsibilities are emotionally heavy.
- Information is split between people.
- Missed tasks can have serious consequences.

### Today must help by

- Prioritising health and care-related commitments.
- Making responsibility visible.
- Surfacing urgent items without overwhelming the user.

## Persona 5: The Low-confidence Technology User

### Profile

A household member who may not be confident with complex apps but still needs to check tasks, events or responsibilities.

### Needs

- Simple language.
- Clear next actions.
- Large touch targets.
- Minimal configuration.
- Trust that the app is safe.

### Pain points

- Complex dashboards feel intimidating.
- Too many options cause hesitation.
- AI can feel unclear or untrustworthy if not explained.

### Today must help by

- Keeping the interface simple.
- Using plain English.
- Making actions obvious.
- Avoiding clutter.

## Cross-persona requirements

Today must support:

- Different household structures.
- Different levels of technical confidence.
- Different caring responsibilities.
- Different daily rhythms.
- Shared and private information.
- Accessibility from the beginning.

## Product implication

Today should be highly adaptive, but never feel complicated. The interface should adjust to the household without making users configure the system manually.
