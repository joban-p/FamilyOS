# 04 Mental Load Analysis

## Purpose

This document explains the mental load that Today is designed to reduce.

Today should not simply aggregate household information. It should reduce the cognitive burden of remembering, checking, prioritising and coordinating everyday life.

## Mental load categories

### 1. Remembering

Users carry many small obligations in their heads.

Examples:

- PE kit.
- Birthday card.
- Bill due tomorrow.
- Medication.
- Pet treatment.
- School trip.
- Insurance renewal.

Today should surface these at the right time so the user does not need to remember them unaided.

### 2. Checking

Users often need to check several systems before they feel prepared.

Examples:

- Calendar.
- Weather.
- Traffic.
- Bank balance.
- Tasks.
- School emails.
- Shared messages.

Today should reduce repeated checking by consolidating relevant context.

### 3. Prioritising

Not everything matters equally.

Users need help understanding what is urgent, important or simply useful.

Today should rank information so the user sees what matters first.

### 4. Coordinating

Household life involves multiple people.

Examples:

- Who is collecting the children?
- Who is feeding the pet?
- Who has the car?
- Who needs to know about a change?
- Who owns this task?

Today should make responsibility and next actions clear.

### 5. Deciding

Decision fatigue begins early.

Examples:

- Should I leave earlier?
- Can we fit this in tonight?
- What should I do first?
- Is this bill urgent?
- Can this wait?

Today should reduce unnecessary decisions by recommending one clear next action when confidence is high.

## Cognitive burden before Today

Without Today, users often start the day with uncertainty.

They may know some commitments but not all of them. They may remember calendar events but not related tasks. They may know a bill is due but not whether the budget can absorb it. They may know a child has an activity but not remember the equipment needed.

This creates stress, even when nothing has gone wrong yet.

## Desired cognitive state after using Today

After using Today, the user should feel:

- I know what matters.
- I know what needs action.
- I know what can wait.
- I trust that important items will be surfaced.
- I can get on with the day.

## Product implication

Today must be selective.

Showing too much information creates more mental load. The product must decide what deserves attention and suppress what does not.

## Primary mental load metric

The main measure is not time spent on Today.

The main measure is confidence gained.

## Success statement

Today succeeds when users stop asking themselves: What have I forgotten?
