# 00 Living Scenario: Monday Morning

## Scenario name

Monday Morning

## Purpose

This Living Scenario defines the emotional and practical benchmark for the Today experience.

Before any designer, engineer or AI agent builds Today, they should understand the real-life moment it is designed to improve.

## The story

It is 6:42am on a Monday.

Sarah's alarm goes off. The house is quiet for another few minutes. She picks up her phone before getting out of bed and opens FamilyOS.

FamilyOS does not show a dashboard full of widgets. It gives her a calm briefing.

Good morning, Sarah.

Today is a little busier than normal.

Leave home by 8:05 because traffic near the school is heavier today. Oliver needs his PE kit. Your energy bill will be taken tomorrow. You are 82 percent of the way towards this month's savings goal. The weather should stay dry until 6pm, so tonight is a good chance for the family walk you have been trying to fit in.

Sarah pauses.

She had not remembered the PE kit.

She had not checked traffic.

She had not thought about the energy bill.

She had not looked at the weather.

FamilyOS had.

She taps Oliver's PE kit reminder and marks it as packed. She taps the family walk suggestion and adds it to the evening if everyone is free.

She closes the app after 24 seconds.

Her day already feels easier.

## Mental load removed

- Remembering school equipment.
- Checking traffic.
- Checking weather.
- Remembering a bill due tomorrow.
- Connecting family goals to available time.
- Deciding what matters first.

## Desired emotional outcome

Sarah should feel prepared, calm and supported before the day begins.

## Product benchmark

If Today cannot make a real Monday morning feel easier, the experience is not good enough.

## Design implication

Today should prioritise useful context over information volume.

The user should never need to hunt for what matters. FamilyOS should identify it, explain it and make the next action obvious.
